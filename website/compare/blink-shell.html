Muxr vs Blink Shell: zellij and herdr on iOS

Last updated: 15 November 2026

Blink Shell is a general SSH and Mosh terminal for iPhone and iPad, and it is open source under GPL-3.0; Muxr is a zellij and herdr client for iOS and Android that needs a daemon on the server. Blink suits you if you want a full terminal on an iPad with a hardware keyboard, and Muxr suits you if you want native session, tab and pane controls for zellij or herdr.

Which one should I pick?

Pick Blink Shell if you want a full SSH and Mosh terminal on iPad with a hardware keyboard, open-source code, or tmux. It needs no daemon on your server (our judgement).

Pick Muxr if you run zellij or herdr, want native screens for their sessions, tabs and panes, or need an Android app as well as an iOS one.

Where is Blink Shell the better choice?

  • You want a "professional, desktop grade terminal" for iOS (blink.sh) that connects over SSH or Mosh to any machine.
  • You want code you can read under an open-source licence: Blink is GPL-3.0 (github.com/blinksh/blink). Muxr is source available.
  • You use tmux. Muxr's tmux support is coming soon.
  • You work on an iPad with a hardware keyboard and want a full terminal rather than a multiplexer client (our judgement).

Where is Muxr the better choice?

  • You use Android. Blink's own site describes it as a terminal for iOS, and Muxr runs on Android 10 and later as well as iOS and iPadOS 15.0 and later.
  • You want to pay once. Muxr Pro is a one-time $19.99 purchase, 50% off ($9.99) until January 13, 2027. Blink lists a yearly price after its free period.
  • You run zellij or herdr and want sessions, tabs and panes as native screens, with an agents list on herdr.
  • You want no account and per-device tokens, including read-only tokens for a view that cannot type.

Can I use both?

Yes. Blink Shell can open a shell on any server, run zellij attach inside it and give you a full terminal on iPhone or iPad. Muxr covers the machines where you have installed muxrd and want native multiplexer controls, and it also runs on Android. They do not share data, and neither needs the other.
