Is Muxr safe? Security and privacy explained

Last updated: 15 November 2026

Muxr sends your terminal traffic straight from your phone to muxrd on your own machine, over TLS, signed in with a per-device token. It does not pass through any server run by F0X IT LLC.

Does my terminal traffic pass through Muxr's servers?

No. The app talks to muxrd, the daemon you run next to zellij or herdr. Keystrokes and output go from the phone to your server and nowhere else. No SSH is used between the app and the server.

How is the connection protected?

The app talks to muxrd over gRPC protected by TLS. The server offers three certificate modes:

  • Self-signed (the default): the server generates its own certificate, and the app pins its SHA-256 fingerprint.
  • External certificate: a certificate from a public or corporate CA that you supply, which the app trusts through the system CA store.
  • Plaintext h2c: unencrypted HTTP/2 for a server that sits behind a TLS-terminating proxy; it refuses a non-loopback bind unless you add an explicit flag.

You pair a phone by scanning a QR code shown by muxrctl. For a self-signed server the code pins the certificate fingerprint; for a CA-signed server the app uses system trust.

How does the app sign in?

With a per-device bearer token. You mint and revoke tokens in muxrctl. The token secret is shown once and kept on the server as a hash, so revoke a token and mint a new one if you lose it.

What can a read-only token do?

A read-only token can navigate tabs, panes and spaces, scroll, and resize its own terminal. It cannot type or otherwise change the session.

There is a deliberate trade-off. Reads are not gated by the read-only check, so a read-only token can list every herdr space and read the tab and pane structure of any space, not just the one its connection is viewing. Pane content still requires an attach, and neither call changes anything or moves focus.

How are saved credentials protected on the phone?

Saved credentials are kept in the iOS Keychain or Android Keystore, gated behind your device biometrics.

Do I need an account, and what does the app collect?

There is no account. The app ships with no analytics, no ads and no crash reporting. Server profiles, preferences and diagnostic logs stay on your device. The privacy policy lists what the website logs and what stays local.

Should I expose muxrd to the internet?

The server binds to 127.0.0.1:50051 by default. A tailnet such as Tailscale is the recommended way to reach it from a phone. Use a public domain for something you would be willing to make public, and read Advanced setup first.

Can I read the code?

Yes, Muxr is source available. The server, muxr-core, and the app, muxr-app, are both under the PolyForm Shield License 1.0.0: free for any use, including at work, except to provide a product that competes with Muxr.

How do I report a vulnerability?

Email support@muxr.app; no separate security address is published. The support page has the details.
