Moshi alternatives for zellij, herdr and tmux on a phone

Last updated: 15 November 2026

Alternatives to Moshi include Muxr, Blink Shell, Termius, Prompt 3, Secure ShellFish, Zelmux, the zellij web client, the Herdr mobile app and Collie. People look for one because of price model, self-hosting, source availability, Android or a specific multiplexer.

This page is published by the makers of Muxr, which is why Muxr is listed first.

Which apps are alternatives to Moshi?

Muxr

Muxr is an iPhone, iPad and Android app that attaches to zellij and herdr sessions through muxrd, a daemon you run on your own machine. It is free with one server; Muxr Pro is a one-time $19.99 purchase, 50% off ($9.99) until January 13, 2027. The app and server are source available under the PolyForm Shield License 1.0.0, with tmux, push notifications and voice input coming soon. Source: Muxr on the App Store and Google Play.

Blink Shell

Blink Shell is described by its makers as “a professional, desktop grade terminal for iOS” for iPhone and iPad. It costs $19.99 per year after 2 weeks free, supports SSH and Mosh, and its code is open source under GPL-3.0. Source: blink.sh and its repository.

Termius

Termius is an SSH client for desktop and mobile. Starter is free and Pro is US$10 per month. It supports SSH, SFTP, Telnet and Mosh. It is a general SSH client, so any multiplexer runs inside the shell session. Source: termius.com and pricing.

Prompt 3

Prompt 3 is made by Panic for iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Vision. It is free with in-app purchases of $9.99 per year or $49.99 one time, with SSH, Mosh and Eternal Terminal. Its store listing covers Apple platforms only. Source: App Store listing.

Secure ShellFish

Secure ShellFish is an SSH terminal with remote file access for iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Vision and Apple Watch. It is free with in-app purchases of $2.99 per month, $14.99 per year or $29.99 lifetime, and has built-in tmux support. It sets up agent hooks for notifications. Its store listing covers Apple platforms only. Source: App Store listing.

Zelmux

Zelmux is a free iPhone and iPad client for zellij, tmux and SSH. It attaches to zellij sessions over HTTPS and WebSocket. Android and herdr are not stated. Source: App Store listing.

The zellij web client

The web client is part of zellij: it lets you attach to existing sessions or start new ones from a browser and starts with zellij web. On phones it offers touch scrolling, on-screen keys and pane and tab pickers. It is free because it ships with zellij, and as a zellij feature it does not attach to herdr. Source: zellij web client documentation.

The Herdr mobile app

The Herdr mobile app, made by the herdr project, is a free iPhone, iPad and Android app that shows which agents need you. It connects over SSH to herdr-core with no Herdr account. Its page says Herdr TUI is not supported and mentions no other multiplexer. Source: herdr.dev/mobile.

Collie

Collie is a self-hosted PWA, MIT-licensed, to drive Claude Code, Pi, Codex and OpenCode in herdr, tmux or zellij from your phone. It opens in the phone’s browser and sends push when an agent pauses or finishes. Source: Collie on GitHub.

When should I stay with Moshi?

Stay with Moshi if you want tmux, push notifications, voice input or Apple Watch support today, or if you do not want to install anything on the server. Its free tier includes a complete terminal, agent monitoring and push notifications with no account. Sources: the Moshi pricing page, the Moshi App Store listing and getmoshi.app. For a side-by-side with Muxr, see Muxr vs Moshi.
